Would you trust a model more if 90% of it was readable?

#2 on HackerNews last week: in a preliminary audit, roughly 90% of the neurons in a 32B model had one main job.

What Goodfire changed to get there:

1. Longer contexts. Short ones made long-context neurons look mixed.

2. Describe the pattern a neuron shares. The famous cat-and-car neuron was detecting a shape.

3. Better metrics. Reward explanations that are right over ones that only mimic activations.

4. Modern layers. Residual connections and LayerNorm keep deep layers from overflowing.

My rule: if I can't read a system, I can't debug it, and models shouldn't get a pass.
