File permissions won't protect your SSH keys. Neither will a password vault.

Serge Gatezh moved his into his Mac's Secure Enclave. The key is created on the chip and never leaves it. Every SSH connection now waits for Touch ID.
---
Plan A, a key file in ~/.ssh: any script that reaches your filesystem can copy it and get into every server. A fake interview repo you clone and run is enough.
---
Plan B, the SSH agent in 1Password or Bitwarden: encrypted at rest, decrypted in memory when used. He wanted a key nobody could extract, even from a fully compromised Mac.
---
What works: sc_auth create-ctk-identity -k p-256-ne -t bio. The key can't be exported, and every signature needs Touch ID, so malware can't use it quietly.

The catch: no backups. Put a YubiKey's key in authorized_keys too.
---
The pushback in the discussion: hardware keys need a human present, which breaks headless AI agents. One developer called that the point. No agent touches his production servers unless his finger is on the key.
