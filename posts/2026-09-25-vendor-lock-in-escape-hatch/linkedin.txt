Three governments. Same operating system. No coordination.

The Dutch government's DAWO project, France's Securix, and Germany's openDesk are three separate efforts to replace the Microsoft desktop stack with declarative, open infrastructure. None of them appears to share a codebase. They landed on the same architecture on their own.

Developers picking apart DAWO agreed the operating system was never the hard part: tools like NixOS make a fleet of machines reproducible from a single config file. The real wall is the office suite. One engineer estimated that cloning Microsoft 365's collaboration features tops out around 80% parity, and argued the useful goal is rethinking the workflow, not building a lookalike of Excel.

If your company dropped a core vendor tomorrow, which would break first: the infrastructure underneath it, or the daily habits built around the tool itself?

Source: DAWO by the DAWO project (dawo.community).
